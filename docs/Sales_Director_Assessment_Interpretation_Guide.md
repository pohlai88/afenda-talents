# Sales Director Interpretation Guide
## Sales Performance & Role Positioning Assessment

### 1. Purpose

This instrument is designed to help a Sales Director understand **how a salesperson is likely to create, progress, protect and expand revenue**, and where that person is most naturally positioned.

It measures job-relevant behaviour and work style. It is **not a clinical psychological test** and it should not be used as the sole basis for hiring, promotion, termination, compensation, or other high-impact employment decisions.

Use it together with:
- objective sales performance;
- role history and territory context;
- structured interview evidence;
- CRM / pipeline behaviour;
- the written battle-test responses;
- references or observed work samples where appropriate.

There is intentionally **no single overall sales score**. A salesperson can be outstanding in one sales motion and poorly matched to another.

---

## 2. The 12 scored dimensions

| Code | Dimension | What a high score generally signals |
|---|---|---|
| HUN | Prospecting Drive & Initiative | Proactive pipeline creation, comfort with rejection and asking for movement |
| DSC | Discovery & Customer Insight | Diagnoses real problems, buying process, stakeholders and decision criteria |
| COM | Commercial Acumen & Value | Converts features to business value; understands economics and deal quality |
| NEG | Negotiation & Closing Judgment | Trades instead of conceding, protects value, asks for commitment |
| RES | Resilience & Competitive Recovery | Recovers from rejection/losses and learns without becoming defensive |
| EXE | Execution & Pipeline Discipline | CRM hygiene, follow-up, next-step discipline and evidence-based forecasting |
| ADP | Learning Agility & Adaptability | Learns quickly, tests approaches and changes tactics when evidence changes |
| REL | Trust, Influence & Customer Orientation | Credibility, listening, constructive tension and long-term customer trust |
| COL | Team Selling & Collaboration | Uses specialists well, coordinates handoffs, shares information and credit |
| AUT | Autonomy & Self-Management | Operates with ownership and limited supervision in ambiguous situations |
| STR | Strategic Account Thinking | Stakeholder mapping, competition, risk, whitespace and account expansion |
| ETH | Ethical Sales Judgment | Accurate claims, early risk escalation and resistance to mis-selling |

### Suggested interpretation of each dimension

| Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 90–100 | Distinctive strength — verify with behavioural evidence because extremely high self-ratings can also reflect impression management |
| 75–89 | Strong |
| 60–74 | Capable |
| 40–59 | Emerging / conditional |
| 0–39 | Development priority |

A low score is not automatically a poor employee. It may indicate a **role mismatch**, a coaching requirement, or a sales environment in which that capability is less central.

---

## 3. Teamwork versus self-work

Do **not** treat collaboration and independence as opposites.

Use `COL` and `AUT` as a two-axis profile:

| COL | AUT | Likely work pattern |
|---|---|---|
| High | High | Independent collaborator — can own outcomes while orchestrating others; attractive for complex / enterprise selling |
| High | Lower | Team-dependent orchestrator — can work well across functions but may benefit from clearer structure or manager cadence |
| Lower | High | Solo producer — may thrive in transactional or self-contained territory roles; test handoff and cross-functional risk |
| Lower | Lower | Structure-dependent contributor — may need a defined process, closer coaching and narrower ownership |

For positioning, “High” can usually be read as **75+**, but use the surrounding evidence rather than a mechanical cut-off.

## 5. Common sales profiles

### The Rainmaker Hunter
Typical pattern: `HUN`, `RES`, `AUT`, `NEG` high.

Good fit:
- new logos;
- difficult territories;
- outbound-heavy motion;
- rapid pipeline creation.

Watch:
- low `EXE` can create forecast chaos;
- low `COL` can create handoff problems;
- low `ETH` is a serious risk regardless of revenue.

### The Consultative Closer
Typical pattern: `DSC`, `COM`, `NEG`, `REL` high.

Good fit:
- higher-value B2B;
- solution sales;
- complex discovery;
- business-case selling.

Watch:
- low `HUN` may make the person dependent on inbound leads;
- low `AUT` may require strong sales management.

### The Strategic Account Builder
Typical pattern: `STR`, `REL`, `COL`, `EXE` high.

Good fit:
- key accounts;
- renewals and expansion;
- multi-business-unit customers;
- long-cycle relationships.

Watch:
- low `NEG` may lead to concessions;
- low `HUN` can reduce new-logo creation.

### The Sales Orchestrator
Typical pattern: `COL`, `STR`, `REL`, `COM` high.

Good fit:
- enterprise;
- channel;
- ecosystem sales;
- situations requiring finance, product, operations and leadership coordination.

Watch:
- low `AUT` can turn coordination into dependency.

### The Solo Producer
Typical pattern: `AUT`, `HUN` high; `COL` lower.

Good fit:
- self-contained territory;
- transactional or shorter-cycle selling;
- roles with few internal dependencies.

Watch:
- complex implementation;
- shared accounts;
- matrix organisations;
- handoff quality.

### The Disciplined Inside Seller
Typical pattern: `EXE`, `HUN`, `RES`, `ADP` high.

Good fit:
- SDR / inside sales;
- high activity;
- repeatable sales motions;
- metric-driven environments.

Watch:
- `DSC` and `COM` must rise before moving into complex solution or enterprise selling.

## 6. Battle-test review rubric

The five required written scenarios are not automatically scored by the JSON engine. Review them as work samples.

Score each criterion from **1 to 5**.

| Criterion | 1 — Weak evidence | 3 — Adequate evidence | 5 — Strong evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prioritisation & sequencing | Random activity; no order | Reasonable plan | Clear order, dependencies, time-boxes and decision points |
| Customer / stakeholder insight | Assumes; pushes product | Some discovery | Tests hypotheses, maps stakeholders, decision criteria and risk |
| Commercial judgment | Discounts / agrees too easily | Protects basics | Quantifies value, trades concessions, protects economics |
| Evidence & metrics | Vague “follow up” | Some measures | Defines conversion evidence, next steps and triggers to change course |
| Team orchestration | Works alone or escalates everything | Uses some support | Involves the right specialists at the right time with clear ownership |
| Integrity & forecast truth | Overpromises / preserves forecast | Mostly accurate | Explicitly separates confirmed facts from assumptions; removes unsupported forecast |
| Learning quality | Defends first idea | Adjusts when prompted | States assumptions, seeks disconfirming evidence and changes course intelligently |

### Scenario-specific signals

**BT-1 — 30-day territory build**
Strong answers show account prioritisation, ICP reasoning, multi-channel sequencing, activity-to-conversion metrics, CRM discipline, qualification and early message testing.

**BT-2 — “Just send a proposal”**
Strong answers resist premature solutioning without becoming obstructive. They identify missing information, preserve momentum and clearly label assumptions.

**BT-3 — Procurement pressure**
Strong answers trade rather than concede, understand the full economic package, use the sponsor intelligently and know their escalation / walk-away boundary.

**BT-4 — Deal rescue**
Strong answers challenge an unsupported forecast, multi-thread the account, mobilise the right internal experts and create a dated recovery path.

**BT-5 — Target versus truth**
Strong answers refuse to represent a future possibility as a confirmed capability while still trying to solve the customer’s underlying requirement commercially.

## 7. How to read contradictions

Contradictions are valuable.

Examples:

- **High HUN + weak BT-1:** confidence may exceed actual prospecting method.
- **High NEG + gives away everything in BT-3:** negotiation self-perception is not supported by demonstrated judgment.
- **High EXE + vague next steps in BT-4:** investigate actual CRM behaviour.
- **High ETH + overpromises in BT-5:** treat as a material discrepancy.
- **High COL + never uses specialists in written cases:** explore whether the person likes teamwork in principle but defaults to solo control under pressure.
- **High AUT + weak prioritisation:** independence may be present without effective self-management.

Do not average contradictions away. Use them to generate structured follow-up questions.

## 8. Response-quality flags

The JSON includes four context checks:

1. **Impression management** — implausibly perfect self-presentation.
2. **Response consistency** — selected paraphrased pairs diverge.
3. **Straight-lining** — the same answer is repeated across a long run.
4. **Rushed completion** — unusually short completion time for this instrument.

A flag is **not evidence of dishonesty**. It means the profile should be interpreted with more behavioural evidence.

---

## 9. Sales Director review sequence

1. **Read the battle tests first.** Form an evidence-based view before seeing self-ratings.
2. **Check response-quality flags.**
3. **Review the 12-dimension profile.**
4. **Compare `COL` and `AUT`** to understand team-versus-independent working style.
5. **Match patterns to the actual sales motion**, not to a generic “best salesperson.”
6. **Compare with objective evidence:** attainment, pipeline creation, conversion, margin, sales cycle, retention, forecast accuracy and CRM hygiene.
7. **Interview the contradictions.**
8. **Position the person** into the role where their strengths create the most value while the role does not depend heavily on their weakest dimensions.
9. **Create 1–3 coaching priorities** rather than trying to develop all low scores at once.
10. **Re-assess after meaningful role exposure or coaching**, not simply because a person disliked their first result.

---

## 10. Recommended decision principle

Use the assessment to answer:

> **“Under which sales motion, level of autonomy, customer complexity and team dependency is this person most likely to perform sustainably?”**

Do not use it to answer:

> **“Is this person psychologically good or bad?”**

The first is role positioning. The second is neither what this instrument measures nor a sound employment decision.
